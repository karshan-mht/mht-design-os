# ThisIsMenopause Brand Source of Truth

## Document goal

Create one canonical markdown document that design, content, PMM, lifecycle, and product teams can use as the shared reference for how **ThisIsMenopause** should look, sound, and behave across site, articles, email, social, paid, and future product touchpoints.

## Recommended document principles

- Be opinionated enough to reduce drift.
- Separate evergreen brand rules from channel-specific execution guidance.
- Distinguish clearly between:
  - brand foundations
  - editorial/copy rules
  - visual system rules
  - UX/product usage rules
  - campaign/channel adaptations
- Include examples, do/don’ts, and links to source assets wherever possible.
- Mark each rule as either:
  - Required
  - Recommended
  - Contextual / channel-specific

## 2. Brand at a glance

### 2.1 Official brand name
Define the official spelling and usage of **ThisIsMenopause** as one word with title-case capitalization, and note that it should not be written as “This Is Menopause” in branded usage.

### 2.2 Core tagline
Document the preferred current tagline and note any conflicts or alternates found in source material:
- Primary: “Making sense of menopause, together.”
- Alternate historical phrasing to preserve but not default to: “Expert guidance and real experiences from women at every stage / every age.”

### 2.3 One-liner / elevator pitch
Capture the current short description:
- “Straight talk, expert guidance, and real experiences for every stage of menopause.”

### 2.4 Brand essence
Include the one-sentence strategic expression of the brand:
- “We make menopause make sense—so women feel informed, supported, and in control.”

### 2.5 Brand promise
Summarize the user promise:
- clarity on what is normal
- practical trustworthy guidance
- real experiences plus expert input

### 2.6 Positioning statement

## 3. Audience definition and jobs to be done

### 3.1 Primary audience
Define the primary initial audience as people in perimenopause, especially those early in the journey and unsure whether what they are experiencing is menopause-related.

### 3.2 Secondary audiences
Capture adjacent audiences:
- postmenopausal people
- premenopausal people who are earlier in the journey and curious what to expect

### 3.3 Emotional starting point
Outline the dominant emotional context:
- confusion
- uncertainty
- dismissal
- overwhelm
- isolation
- desire for reassurance

### 3.4 Functional needs
Document what users most want:
- “Is this normal?” validation
- symptom understanding
- practical management tips
- trusted treatment comparisons
- real experiences
- safe community

### 3.5 Core jobs to be done
Recommend framing around these jobs:
1. Help me understand what is happening.
2. Help me know what is normal.
3. Help me understand my options without judgment.
4. Help me hear from women like me.
5. Help me take a practical next step.

## 5. Voice and tone system

### 5.1 Core voice pillars
Build a durable voice model around:
- reassuring and validating
- bold, direct, and grounded
- practical and action-oriented
- warm, not flippant
- empowering

### 5.2 Tone spectrum
Show where the brand should sit on key axes:
- warm ←→ clinical
- direct ←→ soft
- candid ←→ euphemistic
- hopeful ←→ alarmist
- lightly witty ←→ jokey
- credible ←→ academic

### 5.3 Tone by channel
Specify how tone flexes by surface:
- Product / UX: calmest, clearest, most supportive
- Articles: compassionate, practical, plain-language
- Email: warm and helpful, less embellished
- Social organic: more voice, more irreverence allowed
- Paid: tighter, clearer, emotionally immediate
- PMM / decks: confident and strategic

### 5.4 Voice dos and don’ts
Include operational rules:
- Do normalize experiences.
- Do use simple language and practical takeaways.
- Do acknowledge uncertainty honestly.
- Don’t fear-monger.
- Don’t overpromise outcomes.
- Don’t use heavy medical jargon.
- Don’t shame any path or choice.

### 5.5 Reading level and clarity standard
Adopt the MyHealthTeam guidance for clear, compassionate, plain language and target roughly an 8th grade reading level for consumer-facing content.

## 7. Copywriting rules and language guardrails

### 7.1 Naming and capitalization rules
Include exact formatting rules for:
- ThisIsMenopause
- URL usage
- bolding / emphasis rules when needed

### 7.2 Approved language patterns
Document preferred patterns such as:
- “what’s normal”
- “make sense of menopause”
- “clear answers”
- “practical tips”
- “real experiences”
- “expert-reviewed” / “expert guidance”
- “judgment-free”
- “supportive community”
- “symptom relief” where appropriate

### 7.3 Restricted or avoid language
Document explicit avoid lists:
- cure / fix / eliminate symptom claims
- shame-based framing
- overly negative or “everything sucks” framing
- panic / urgency language
- one-size-fits-all treatment claims
- weight-loss-led headlines
- polarizing anti-HRT or pro-HRT absolutism

### 7.4 Medical terminology rules
Include a Menopause-specific editorial section pulling from the MHT style guide, especially:
- use **menopause hormone therapy** on first reference
- “hormone therapy” is OK afterward
- avoid relying on “hormone replacement therapy” or “HRT” except for SEO or orientation in intros

### 7.5 Inclusive language rules
Include the MHT guidance adapted for this brand:
- default to gender-neutral language in editorial guidance where possible
- use the precise source language when accuracy requires gendered terms
- avoid unnecessary assumptions about identity

### 7.6 Plain-language conversion rules
Include a short Menopause-specific version of the simple-language chart for common topics:
- symptom vs sign
- menopause hormone therapy vs HRT
- “high blood pressure” vs “hypertension”
- “doctor” vs “clinician”
- “what you need to know” vs “overview”

### 7.7 Structural writing rules
Add formatting standards for readable copy:
- short paragraphs
- clear subheads
- bullets where useful
- practical takeaway emphasis
- internal and external source-linking standards for article content teams
